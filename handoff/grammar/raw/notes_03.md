## 1. English prepositions ↔ Burmese postpositions (vocabulary table)

Source: 1Ld-lvyBCU4Henj_uW49sieVjqnBHGU4L (frame 14; it comes from a different video/slide set: black background, plain table)

The slide pairs English prepositions with their Burmese equivalents. In Burmese these are **postpositions/particles that come after the noun**, where English puts the preposition before it.

| English | Burmese (as shown) | Romanisation (mine) | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| since | ကတည်းက | ka.ta-de:-ga. | "since / from (a time) on"; comes after a time noun |
| at | မှာ | hma | location "at/in/on" |
| under | အောက်မှာ | auk-hma | "below/under" = အောက် (underneath) + မှာ (at) |
| to | သို့ | thou. | "to/towards" (formal/literary, the written form of ကို when it marks direction) |
| from | မှ | hma. | "from" (formal/literary; the spoken form is က) |

Pattern (inferred, explained by me rather than read from the slide): **Noun + postposition**, e.g. အိမ်မှာ "at home", စားပွဲအောက်မှာ "under the table", ၂၀၂၀ ကတည်းက "since 2020", ရန်ကုန်မှ "from Yangon". These example phrases are my own (inferred); they are not on the slide.

### Overview (frame 20)
**Opinion adjectives** split into two groups:
- **General adjectives**: can describe almost any noun.
- **Specific adjectives**: can describe only certain kinds of noun.

**Rule (frames 1, 2, 21, 24, 26):** when both kinds come before a noun, **the general opinion adjective goes first and the specific one second**.
Formula: **(a/an) + general adjective + specific adjective + noun**

| Example (English, as shown) | Burmese | Note |
|---|---|---|
| nice tasty food | — (not shown) | nice = general, tasty = specific (food → tasty, nice; frame 1) |
| nice tasty soup | — | |
| nice tasty dinner | — | |
| nice tasty pizza | — | |
| a nasty uncomfortable armchair | — | shown with a picture of an awkward red chair |
| a lovely intelligent cat | — | shown with a picture of a white kitten |

### General adjectives (frames 3–7)
Positive: good, lovely, nice, beautiful, brilliant, wonderful, excellent
Negative/other: bad, nasty, awful, dreadful, horrible, important, strange

| Word | Burmese gloss shown | Romanisation (mine) |
|---|---|---|
| strange | ထူးဆန်းသော | htu:-hsan:-dhaw |

Examples showing that general adjectives work with any noun:
- an important person / an important situation / an important position
- a nasty person / a nasty situation (frames 22–23)

### Specific adjectives (frames 8–19)
These only fit certain nouns. Wrong combinations are marked with ✗ on the slides.

| Adjective | Burmese gloss shown | Romanisation (mine) | Correct (✓) | Wrong (✗) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| tasty | — | — | tasty food, tasty soup, tasty meal, tasty dinner | a tasty person, a tasty boy, a tasty girl, a tasty teacher, a tasty situation |
| comfortable / uncomfortable | — | — | a comfortable chair, a comfortable sofa, a comfortable income | an uncomfortable person |
| clever | တော်သော | taw-dhaw | clever dog, clever son | clever situation, clever bed |
| friendly | ဖော်ရွေသော | hpaw-ywe-dhaw | friendly dog, friendly cat, friendly person, friendly teacher | friendly mirror |

Burmese point that comes along with the glosses (inferred, explained by me rather than stated on the slide): Burmese adjectives used before a noun are formed as **verb/adjective stem + သော** (formal/written attributive marker, colloquial = တဲ့), e.g. တော် "be clever" → တော်သော "clever". In word order this is **[adj+သော] + noun**, e.g. တော်သော ခွေး "a clever dog" (inferred example).
